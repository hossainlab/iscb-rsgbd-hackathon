# Code of Conduct

**1st Bioinformatics Hackathon 2026, RSG Bangladesh**
(Regional Student Group under the ISCB Student Council)

This hackathon brings small teams together to build useful and well-documented bioinformatics tools, in the open, in a short time. It is a collaborative event, not a contest between people. This Code of Conduct describes how we expect everyone to behave so that every participant can learn, contribute, and be credited fairly.

## 1. Who this applies to

Everyone taking part in any capacity: participants, team leads, mentors, organizers, judges, speakers, sponsors, volunteers, and guests.

It applies in every hackathon space:

* the venue, including meals, breaks, and social gatherings linked to the event
* online spaces: chat channels, video calls, email, shared documents
* the hackathon GitHub organization: repositories, issues, pull requests, reviews, and commit messages
* public posts about the event, including social media

It applies before the event (applications, kickoff call), during the event, and afterwards (follow-up work, the hackathon report).

## 2. Expected behavior

* **Be respectful.** Treat every person with courtesy regardless of background, institution, or skill level.
* **Be collaborative.** Share what you know. Ask for help early. Help other teams when you can; their success is not your loss.
* **Be inclusive of all skill levels.** Teams are deliberately mixed. No question is too basic. Explain rather than take over the keyboard.
* **Be constructive.** Criticize ideas and code, never people. Offer a suggestion along with the critique.
* **Be reliable.** Show up on time, do what you said you would do, and tell your team lead early if you cannot.
* **Be open.** Work in the team's public repository, document what you do, and keep discussions in shared channels so nobody is left out.
* **Be honest.** Report what works and what does not. A clear negative result is a valid hackathon outcome.
* **Look after yourself and others.** Take breaks, eat, drink water, and tell an organizer if someone seems to be struggling.

## 3. Unacceptable behavior

The following are not tolerated:

* Harassment, intimidation, or discrimination based on gender, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, disability, physical appearance, age, race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, language, regional origin, socioeconomic background, institution, academic level, or technical experience.
* Sexual language or imagery in any hackathon space, and unwelcome sexual attention or physical contact.
* Insults, demeaning jokes, personal attacks, or belittling someone for what they do not know.
* Deliberately excluding a teammate from the work, discussions, or credit.
* Sustained disruption of talks, demos, or another team's work.
* Stalking, following, or photographing or recording someone without their consent.
* Sharing another person's private information without permission.
* Retaliating against anyone who reports a concern in good faith.
* Encouraging or assisting any of the above.

## 4. Scientific and technical integrity

* **Original and attributed work.** Do not present someone else's code, data, figures, or text as your own. Cite the tools, datasets, and papers you build on, and respect their licenses.
* **Open source by default.** Code produced during the hackathon is developed in a public repository under the hackathon organization and released under an open-source license (MIT unless the team documents another OSI-approved license in its repository).
* **Responsible data use.** Use only data you have the right to use. Do not bring or upload identifiable human data, unpublished data belonging to a lab without that lab's permission, or data whose terms forbid redistribution.
* **No fabrication.** Do not invent or alter results, benchmarks, or demo output.
* **Disclose AI assistance.** AI coding and writing tools may be used. Teams are responsible for the correctness and licensing of anything such a tool produces, and should state in their README where these tools were used.
* **Fair credit.** Everyone who contributed to a project is listed in its README, including contributors of documentation, testing, figures, and domain knowledge. Authorship on any report or publication arising from the hackathon is agreed by the team in the open and reflects actual contribution.
* **Respect shared resources.** Use the venue, network, and any shared compute only for hackathon work, and do not attempt to access systems or data you are not authorized to use.

## 5. Photography and media

The event may be photographed or recorded for RSG Bangladesh and ISCB Student Council reporting and outreach. Tell an organizer at registration if you do not want to appear in published photos or video, and that will be respected. Ask before photographing individuals or another team's screens.

## 7. How reports are handled

Organizers will listen, record what happened, and act as promptly as the situation requires. Anyone with a conflict of interest steps aside from the decision. Depending on severity, the response may be:

1. a private conversation and a request to stop
2. a formal warning
3. reassignment to another team or removal from a session
4. removal from the hackathon without eligibility for certificates or awards
5. exclusion from future RSG Bangladesh events, and where appropriate a report to the person's institution or to the ISCB Student Council

Anyone asked to stop a behavior is expected to comply immediately.

## 8. Agreement

By registering for or attending the hackathon, you agree to follow this Code of Conduct. Team leads and mentors are expected to model it and to raise problems with the organizers early.
